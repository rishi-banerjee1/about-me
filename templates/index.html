Frameworks & practice

Frameworks, talent & systems

My work on interview frameworks, AI-native talent, and recruiting systems starts with understanding what great contribution looks like, then helping people recognise and develop it.

Find and assess AI-native talent

The search starts with what the business needs someone to build. For production AI roles, the assessment follows one system through real use: what the person shipped, owned, operated, measured, and learned.

A written standard makes the required evidence clear. Alongside capability, I examine how someone holds a reasoned view, responds to better evidence, and grows as the work changes.

My approach to finding and assessing AI-native talent

Define great, then build the interview

What would great contribution look like here: in a role, across a team, or for an entire function? That is where I start with leaders. We connect each hire to the capability the organisation needs and how people can make one another stronger. That is how I think about raising Talent Density.

With leaders, I work backwards from what the business needs, examine examples of exceptional contribution, and agree on the few differences that matter. We compare our interpretations before putting that definition into a shared success profile.

Much of my work is helping interviewers explore that contribution: listening closely, following a useful line of questioning, and making sense of what they hear. Reviewing interviews and comparing assessments gives us something real to learn from.

I built Interview Evaluator to turn interview transcripts into specific feedback on strengths and areas to practise in the next conversation.

Explore hiring and interview architecture

Build AI-native recruiting systems

My approach to recruiting systems starts with a recurring decision: what context it needs, what the system can prepare, and where a person must exercise judgment.

RishiOS connects role definition, assessment, notes, and calibration through a shared hiring framework. TA CentCom makes search constraints, ownership, and the next action visible.

The design question remains practical: can a hiring team inspect the evidence, question the output, and act on it? The hiring decision stays with the people accountable for it.
